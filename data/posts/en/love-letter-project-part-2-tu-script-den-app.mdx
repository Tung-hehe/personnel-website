In [part one](/en/blog/love-letter-project), Love Letter was built just to send love letters - the 11
templates back then were all about romance. But what I actually loved wasn't "writing love letters," it
was sitting down and putting hard-to-say things into words - and that isn't unique to romance. So Love
Letter stopped being just about "love letters" <Twemoji emoji="sparkles" />.

## No longer just about two people

From 11 templates built purely for romance, the project is now up to 19: friendship, siblings,
"comrades," family (3/4/5-person versions), farewell, celebration. The name "Love Letter" started
feeling too narrow too, so the app got renamed to **Thư Tay** ("handwritten letter") - fit for any
occasion.

## The CLI wasn't enough anymore

When a letter only ever went to one person, making them install Python was tolerable. But once letters
could go to parents, friends, coworkers, that technical barrier worked against the whole point.

So I built a web app with Astro + TypeScript, deployed at
[love-letter.tungtt.dev](https://love-letter.tungtt.dev/): open the link and start writing, nothing to
install. To run entirely in the browser with no backend, I ported all 19 Jinja2 templates to a small
Jinja2-like interpreter written in TypeScript (`tiny-jinja.ts`). Because of that, your letter never
leaves your device: no server, no account, nobody can read what you write.

## A smoother writing flow, for any occasion

The app now has a **Library** that saves multiple letters right in the browser (no account, no cloud).
The compose screen has a title, date, greeting, paragraph-based content (with manual page breaks), a
closing, a signature, and a postscript. On the **style picker**, every template ships with a preview in
its own voice - so you get a feel for the mood before writing; pick a style for an empty letter and that
sample text fills in as a starting point.

Desktop gets a live preview pane right next to the form; mobile gets a dedicated preview screen before
you download or share.

## Sending it off is still just as simple

The output is still **a single `.html` file**, sendable through any chat app. Mobile now has a Share
button that opens the native share sheet directly, no manual download needed.

The CLI is still there for anyone who wants to dig deeper, plus a new `generate_all.py` script that
renders one letter against all 19 templates at once for comparison.

## Closing thoughts

Something that started because my handwriting is bad and I wanted to send a nice love letter is now a
small app my family and friends use too - for anything hard to say out loud, to anyone.
